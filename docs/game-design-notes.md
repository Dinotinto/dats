# DATS workshop

Choose the blue or red build, then browse the horizontal parts shelf by bodies, weapons, gadgets or wheels. Drag a card onto a matching socket: hexagons take weapons, squares take defence/gadgets and circles take wheels. Empty sockets remain visible; occupied icons hide until the fitted part is hovered, clicked or touched. Clicking a fitted part selects it and retains its mount icon until another selection or fitting operation. Click its artwork, socket, or fitted shelf card, then use **Remove this part** to detach that weapon, gadget, defence or wheel. Both wheels must be fitted before fighting. The chassis remains the backbone and can be replaced. Each category remembers its shelf position.

Dragging near a compatible socket displays the actual part artwork at its mount anchor and orientation before release, and highlights that target. The floating card preview disappears while the mounted preview is active. The preview turns red if fitting would overload energy. Clicking a spare part automatically fits an empty compatible slot, respecting an explicitly selected socket; forklifts and other contact gadgets favour low front mounts, while boosters favour rear mounts. Garage rebuilds preserve the car's position, rotation and suspension frame rather than recentering it on every selection.

Different blueprints may use the same weapon type. Within one car, each fitted weapon requires a distinct collected item UID. Starter inventory contains exactly one copy of each weapon. Use Buy another copy at the selected part’s current tier, or earn copies in supply boxes, to fit duplicates. Fitting or removing an item affects only that blueprint. Upgrading an item updates any blueprint referencing it. Equipped parts in either blueprint are protected from fusion sacrifice. Player and chassis tiers, mount type and energy determine whether a part can be used.

The garage uses an original warm workshop backdrop, illustrated inventory cards and compatible socket highlights while dragging. The car fills the area below the shelf; its driver selection, health, combined upgraded attack rating, energy and upgrade controls sit at the right in a vertically scrollable panel. The attack tooltip explains contact damage per second and projectile damage per hit. Select **Upgrade part** to expand fusion and toolbox controls. The preview automatically fits tall chassis and long weapons within the available space and retains active suspension physics.

The selected cat stands beside the garage car with a wrench and briefly works on it whenever the build refreshes. The cockpit is empty in the workshop; the cat returns to its seat in battle. Mounts are original illustrated metal plates with bolts and distinct illuminated hexagonal weapon, square gadget, and circular wheel inserts. Occupied and selected mounts have visual cues. A fitted part now drags with its own illustrated icon.

Chassis equipment capacity grows with its tier: tier 1 has up to two attack and two defence/gadget mounts, tier 2 three/two, tier 3 three/three, tier 4 four/three and tier 5 four/four. Two wheel mounts are additional. Titan follows these rules too; its former four-weapon starter advantage has been removed. Chassis upgrades are gated by match-earned player progression, and refreshing an upgraded body reveals the new physical mounts. Higher tiers retain four/four. Mount positions remain specific to each body shape. Titan retains a low front defence mount; Classic, Surfer, Whale and Pyramid also have front gadget positions. Original bodies accept gadgets on their front defence sockets. Side-mounted gadgets render over wheel hubs.

The energy meter sums level-adjusted weapons and gadgets; wheels cost zero. Level-one weapons cost 2–3 energy and gadgets cost 2. A weapon or gadget costs one additional energy per tier, while chassis capacity gains four per tier. A power toolbox adds one permanent chassis capacity. Overloads, missing wheels and incompatible tiers disable Fight. Gadget health contributes to total car health alongside the body and tyres. All parts stay active until total health reaches zero; actual spike contact defeats the entire vehicle instantly, even before walls move at 12 seconds.

Select a part and expand **Upgrade part**. Fusion consumes a spare and gold, adds rarity-weighted XP and raises its equipment tier, up to the unlocked tier. Health doubles with each tier. All damaging weapons fit these attack bands, including magic and permanent attack bonuses: tier 1 = 40–100, tier 2 = 100–200, tier 3 = 200–400, tier 4 = 400–800, tier 5 = 800–1,600. Bands double thereafter through tier 10. The level-one laser hits for 100 and costs 3 energy. Melee ratings are DPS while touching; projectile ratings are damage per hit. Minigun rating is maximum DPS, with each round dealing 10% and the firing rate ramping from four to ten rounds per second. Grappling hooks remain zero-damage utility. Attack toolboxes add five, capped by the tier; health toolboxes add 25, and power adds one energy.

Player levels 1–10 unlock tier 1, 11–20 unlock tier 2, continuing to tier 10 at player levels 91–100. A weapon, wheel or gadget also requires a chassis of at least its tier. The blue build is the local player's car. Wins award 120 player XP and 100 gold; losses/draws award 60 XP and 50 gold. Every third win grants a free supply box. Player level requirements start at 100 XP and increase by 20 each level. Supply boxes cost 150 gold unless a free one is available, and contain three parts from unlocked tiers plus a toolbox. The upgrade panel also lets players buy the selected part at the next unlocked tier for 150 × tier² gold. Rewards and limits live in `scripts/progression.gd` for later balancing.

Inventory, currency, player level/XP, wins, free boxes, immutable body layout seeds and upgrades are saved to Godot's `user://garage_v2.json`. Existing version-two saves remain readable; older fused tiers establish a matching unlocked player tier when no progression profile exists. These progression rules and rewards currently run locally. Online accounts, server-validated rewards and matchmaking remain work for launch.

Cars keep at least 72% of their wheel target speed while pressing together and drive 25% faster. Crusher spike tips start at 1052 units from centre, leaving about 722 units from either spawn centre. The 45-second fight warns at 9 seconds and closes the walls from 12 seconds onward. Each individual spike uses a current-position shape query; only a car part intersecting a visible triangle is destroyed. The damage event appears at that spike tip.

Cats show concern below 35% health and panic below 15%. Defeat ejects the selected cat with a complete lower body, moving legs, boots and tail. A parachute opens and limits descent; feet meet the arena floor on landing.

Contact gadgets use their visible artwork with a two-unit physics contact tolerance, so solid collision response cannot prevent activation. Lifter and Repulse have larger visible working faces that extend beyond front-mounted hulls. A lifter attaches a visible balloon to the actual opponent it touches, launches its whole assembly, and supplies controlled buoyancy for 3.5 seconds before normal falling resumes. Repulse pushes all linked enemy bodies away and recoils the owner, with a shockwave at the fitted gadget. Forklifts articulate anticlockwise on the left and clockwise on the right, lifting the complete opponent on contact. Backpedal retreats with its wheel motors and brakes near walls; booster provides a start burst. Harpoon must land its projectile before its cable reels the opponent in. First aid restores 25% health once below 50%; armour and shields add passive health and remain fitted throughout the fight. Mount height and orientation still determine whether contact gadgets can reach an opponent.

Harpoon fires its first shot shortly after battle starts, while a gap remains to pull closed, then uses its regular four-second firing cooldown. Run `--headless --path game --fixed-fps 60 --script res://tools/gadget_live_checks.gd` for actual driving encounters involving lifters and repulses on both sides, including a Titan with heavy metal wheels, plus real booster starts and harpoon impacts and reeling. The checks cover activation, complete-assembly lifting, the full balloon lifetime and restored falling; `movement_checks.gd` covers articulated forklift, retreat and winch physics. `gadget_preview.gd` captures the working effects to screenshots.

From the project root, use the Godot console executable with `--headless --path game --script res://tools/combat_checks.gd`, `progression_checks.gd`, `equipment_checks.gd`, or `garage_checks.gd -- --isolated`. The garage checks exercise native mouse dragging in both directions. Run `--headless --path game --fixed-fps 60 --script res://tools/expansion_battles.gd` for the eight new chassis matchups, or `--headless --path game --fixed-fps 60 -- --autotest` for legacy battle regressions. Preview scripts write images to the repository's screenshots folder.


Three control weapons occupy attack sockets: Balloon Launcher, Net Launcher and Flipper Net. Their projectiles must physically hit; misses have no effect and these tools cause no direct damage. A balloon projectile applies buoyancy at its actual impact point for five seconds, so nose hits create nose-up torque rather than raising every body equally. The ordinary net stops movement and weapon use for five seconds. The flipper net turns the complete assembly onto its back, restrains it for five seconds, then raises and rights it during a short recovery before release. Existing parts remain attached and resume operating after release. Restraints cannot stack or refresh; a one-second recovery immunity prevents immediate repeated trapping. Net victims can still take damage. The original contact Lifter retains its 3.5-second whole-assembly lift. Forklift artwork and matching physical forks now have 30% more reach.

Run `control_checks.gd` for copy ownership, tier slots, projectile hit/miss tests, impact-point lift, five-second effects, disabled attacks, flipper recovery and actual driving encounters from both sides. `control_preview.gd` captures the new effects.
